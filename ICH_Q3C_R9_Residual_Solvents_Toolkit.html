Guidelines — how residual-solvent control works

Purpose & scope

ICH Q3C recommends acceptable amounts of residual solvents in pharmaceuticals for patient safety. Residual solvents are organic volatile chemicals used or produced in the manufacture of drug substances or excipients, or in the preparation of drug products, that are not completely removed by practical manufacturing techniques.

  • Applies to drug substances, excipients and drug products — test only for solvents used or produced in manufacture/purification.
  • A cumulative method may be used to calculate drug-product levels from ingredient levels; if the calculated level is ≤ the recommended limit, no drug-product testing is needed.
  • Does not apply to clinical-research-stage materials or existing marketed products; all dosage forms and routes are in scope.
  • Higher levels may be acceptable for short-term use (≤30 days) or topical application — justified case by case.

Classification of residual solvents (Section 3.1)

CLASS 1

Solvents to be avoided

Known or strongly suspected human carcinogens, and environmental hazards. May only be used with strong risk–benefit justification; limits per Table 1.

CLASS 2

Solvents to be limited

Non-genotoxic animal carcinogens, agents of other irreversible toxicity (neurotoxicity, teratogenicity), or other significant but reversible toxicities. Limited via PDE (Table 2).

CLASS 3

Low toxic potential

≤ 50 mg/day acceptable without justification (5000 ppm / 0.5% under Option 1); higher amounts allowed if realistic vs. manufacturing capability & GMP (Table 3).

TABLE 4

No adequate toxicological data

May be of interest, but no PDE could be based on the data. Manufacturers must justify residual levels of these solvents.

Key concept: Permitted Daily Exposure (PDE)

PDE is the pharmaceutically acceptable intake of a residual solvent per day (mg/day). It is derived from the NOEL (or LOEL) of the most relevant animal study (Appendix 3):

PDE (mg/day) = (NOEL or LOEL (mg/kg/day) × body-weight adjustment (kg)) / (F1 × F2 × F3 × F4 × F5)

Body weight is taken as an arbitrary 50 kg (adds a built-in safety margin vs. 60–70 kg; a lower weight should be used for pediatric products). 100% systemic exposure is assumed regardless of route. Modifying factors:

F1 — species extrapolationValue
Rat → human5
Mouse → human12
Dog → human2
Rabbit → human2.5
Monkey → human3
Other animals → human10

F2 = 10: inter-individual variability (used consistently for all organic solvents). F5 = 1 if a NOEL was established; up to 10 if only an LOEL is available (severity-dependent).

F3 — study durationValue
≥ half lifetime (1 yr rodent/rabbit; 7 yr cat/dog/monkey); repro studies covering full organogenesis1
6-month rodent / 3.5-year non-rodent2
3-month rodent / 2-year non-rodent5
Shorter duration10
F4 — severity of toxicityValue
No severe toxicity; fetal toxicity with maternal toxicity1
Fetal toxicity without maternal toxicity; teratogenic effect with maternal toxicity; non-genotoxic carcinogenicity / neurotoxicity / teratogenicity5
Teratogenic effect without maternal toxicity; oncogenic effect10

Two options for Class 2 limits (Section 3.3)

Concentration (ppm) = (1000 × PDE (mg/day)) / dose (g/day)

Option 1 — use the fixed Table 2 ppm limits, calculated with an assumed daily product mass of 10 g. Valid for any formulation provided the daily dose ≤ 10 g; if all components meet Option 1, no further calculation is needed.

Option 2 — use the PDE (mg/day) with the known maximum daily dose and equation (1). Sum the solvent amount contributed by each component per day — the total must stay below the PDE. Products dosed at > 10 g/day must be considered under Option 2.

If Option 2 fails after summation: test the drug product (formulation may reduce the level) → reduce the solvent in manufacture → only in exceptional cases, submit a summary of reduction efforts plus a risk–benefit analysis.

Analytical procedures & reporting (Sections 3.4–3.5)

What is new in R9 (24 Jan 2024): a minor revision to Section 3.4 clarifying that the impact of solvent volatility on the test method should be considered during validation of residual-solvent methods.
  • Residual solvents are typically determined by gas chromatography; use harmonised pharmacopoeial procedures if feasible, otherwise a validated alternative.
  • If only Class 3 solvents are present, a non-specific method such as loss on drying may be used if properly validated.
  • Method validation should conform to ICH Q2 (Validation of Analytical Procedures).
  • Suppliers may report: only Class 3 likely → LoD < 0.5%; Class 2 X, Y,… all below Option 1 limits (with or without Class 3 < 0.5%). Class 1 solvents, if likely, must be identified and quantified. Solvents above their Option 1 limit (or 0.5% for Class 3) must be identified and quantified.
  • “Likely to be present” = used in the final manufacturing step, or used earlier and not consistently removed by a validated process.

Calculators — live, with shown working

All formulas and default values are taken from ICH Q3C(R9) (Section 3.3, Appendix 3, and PDE derivations in Parts II–VI). Results update live as you type.

1 · Concentration limit from daily dose (Option 1 / Option 2)

Equation (1): ppm = (1000 × PDE) ÷ dose (g/day). Option 1 assumes a 10 g/day product mass; enter your own daily dose for Option 2.

—

2 · Cumulative daily exposure — Option 2 summation across components

Sum the solvent contributed by each component (drug substance + excipients). Exposure per component (mg/day) = amount (g) × content (ppm) ÷ 1000. Pass = total ≤ PDE.

—

3 · PDE derivation from a toxicity study (Appendix 3)

PDE = (NOEL/LOEL × 50 kg) ÷ (F1 × F2 × F3 × F4 × F5) with F2 fixed at 10. Also gives the Option 1 ppm limit and an indicative class assignment (PDE ≥ 50 mg/day → Class 3 candidate, per the Part II–VI logic).

—

4 · Inhalation study → daily dose (ppm → mg/kg/day)

The three-step conversion used for the THF, cumene, TEA and MIBK PDE derivations: ppm → mg/m³ (ideal gas, 24.45 L/mol) → continuous-exposure concentration → daily dose.

Physiological defaults from Table A3.1: rat 290 L/day & 0.425 kg; mouse 43 L/day & 0.028 kg; rabbit 1440 L/day & 4 kg; human 28,800 L/day.

—

5 · Unit converter — ppm ⇄ mg/m³ (ideal gas)

mg/m³ = ppm × MW ÷ 24.45 (1 atm, 25 °C); the guideline uses the same relation for ppm → mg/L (÷ 24,450 for mg/L since 1000 L = 1 m³).

Case studies & worked examples from the guideline

Every example below reproduces the official numbers from ICH Q3C(R9) — the Section 3.3 summation examples and the PDE derivations in Parts II–VI. Use the calculator buttons to replay them.

Case 1 · Acetonitrile — Option 2 summation that passes

Section 3.3

PDE for acetonitrile = 4.1 mg/day (Option 1 limit 410 ppm). Product daily mass 5.0 g; the drug substance and excipient 2 exceed the Option 1 limit, but the summed daily exposure stays below the PDE — the product conforms.

ComponentAmount in formulationAcetonitrile contentDaily exposure
Drug substance0.3 g800 ppm0.24 mg
Excipient 10.9 g400 ppm0.36 mg
Excipient 23.8 g800 ppm3.04 mg
Drug product5.0 g728 ppm3.64 mg ✓

Result: 3.64 mg/day ≤ PDE 4.1 mg/day → conforms to the guideline.

Case 2 · Acetonitrile — Option 2 summation that fails

Section 3.3

Same 5.0 g product, but excipient 1 contains 2000 ppm acetonitrile. The total now exceeds both the Option 1 and Option 2 limits.

ComponentAmount in formulationAcetonitrile contentDaily exposure
Drug substance0.3 g800 ppm0.24 mg
Excipient 10.9 g2000 ppm1.80 mg
Excipient 23.8 g800 ppm3.04 mg
Drug product5.0 g1016 ppm5.08 mg ✗

Result: 5.08 mg/day > PDE 4.1 mg/day → fails. Remedy path per guideline: test the drug product (formulation may reduce the level) → reduce solvent in manufacture → exceptional case: risk–benefit summary.

Case 3 · Tetrahydrofuran (THF) — reclassified Class 3 → Class 2

Part II · PDE 7.2 mg/day

Former PDE was 121 mg/day (Class 3). NTP 2-year inhalation studies (rats & mice, 6 h/day, 5 days/week) showed carcinogenic activity (kidney tumours in male rats; liver tumours in female mice). PDE derived from the lowest exposure in the most sensitive species, male rat at 200 ppm:

200 ppm THF (MW 72.10) → 200 × 72.10 / 24.45 = 589.8 mg/m³ = 0.59 mg/L continuous dosing → 0.59 × (6/24) × (5/7) = 0.105 mg/L daily dose → 0.105 × 290 / 0.425 = 71.65 mg/kg/day PDE = 71.65 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 1 × 10 × 1) = 7.165 ≈ 7.2 mg/day Option 1 limit = 7.2 × 1000 / 10 = 720 ppm

F1=5 (rat), F2=10, F3=1 (2-year), F4=10 (oncogenic effect), F5=1 (NOEL established). THF moved into Class 2 as a non-genotoxic carcinogen.

Case 4 · Cumene — reclassified Class 3 → Class 2

Part IV · PDE 0.70 mg/day

Former PDE 55 mg/day (Class 3). NTP 2-year inhalation studies showed rodent carcinogenicity. Calculated from female mouse LOEL 125 ppm (more conservative than the 150 ppm rat LOEL):

Mouse: 125 ppm × 120.19 / 24.45 = 614 mg/m³ = 0.61 mg/L 0.61 × (6/24) × (5/7) = 0.11 mg/L → 0.11 × 43 / 0.028 = 169 mg/kg/day PDE = 169 × 50 / (12 × 10 × 1 × 10 × 10) = 0.70 mg/day → Limit 70 ppm

F1=12 (mouse), F3=1 (105 weeks), F4=10 (oncogenic), F5=10 (NOEL not established — LOEL used). Cumene moved to Class 2.

Case 5 · Methylisobutylketone (MIBK) — Class 3 → Class 2, two scenarios converge

Part V · PDE 45 mg/day

Former PDE 100 mg/day (Class 3). NTP 2-year inhalation data (IARC group 2B). Two independent calculations both give ~45 mg/day: scenario 1 uses female-rat CPN LOEL 450 ppm; scenario 2 uses the tumour NOEL 900 ppm.

Scenario 1 (LOEL 450 ppm, CPN): 450 × 100.16 / 24.45 = 1843 mg/m³ = 1.843 mg/L 1.843 × (6/24) × (5/7) = 0.329 mg/L → 0.329 × 290 / 0.425 = 225 mg/kg/day PDE = 225 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 1 × 1 × 5) = 45 mg/day → Limit 4500 ppm Scenario 2 (NOEL 900 ppm, tumours): → 449 mg/kg/day PDE = 449 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 1 × 10 × 1) = 44.9 ≈ 45 mg/day → Limit 4490 ppm

Mouse liver tumours were CAR-mediated — not relevant to humans — so no mouse-based PDE was calculated. MIBK placed in Class 2.

Case 6 · Triethylamine (TEA) — stays Class 3

Part V · PDE 62.5 mg/day

Sub-chronic inhalation study in F344 rats: NOEL 247 ppm (28 weeks, 6 h/day, 5 days/week), no significant treatment-related effects.

247 × 101.19 / 24.45 = 1022.2 mg/m³ = 1.022 mg/L 1.022 × (6/24) × (5/7) = 0.183 mg/L → 0.183 × 290 / 0.425 = 124.9 mg/kg/day PDE = 124.9 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 2 × 1 × 1) = 62.5 mg/day Limit (illustrative) = 62.5 × 1000 / 10 = 6250 ppm

F3=2 (28 weeks). Since PDE > 50 mg/day, TEA stays in Class 3 (“low toxic potential”) — no health-based limit required, but ≤ 0.5% / 5000 ppm under Option 1.

Case 7 · N-Methylpyrrolidone (NMP) — continues at Class 2

Part III · PDE 5.3 mg/day

Prenatal inhalation study in Wistar rats (150 ppm, 6 h/day, GD 7–20): developmental neurotoxicity (impaired pre-weaning development, higher-cognitive deficits); NOEL not established.

150 × 99.13 / 24.45 = 608.16 mg/m³ = 0.608 mg/L 0.608 × 6/24 = 0.152 mg/L → 0.152 × 290 / 0.33 = 133.58 mg/kg/day PDE = 133.58 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 1 × 5 × 5) = 5.3 mg/day → Limit 530 ppm

F1=5 (rat), F3=1 (repro study covering organogenesis), F4=5 (fetal toxicity without maternal toxicity), F5=5 (LOEL). NMP remains in Class 2.

Case 8 · Part VI trio — 2-MTHF, CPME and TBA

Parts VI · 2021
2-Methyltetrahydrofuran50 mg/dayClass 3 · NOEL 250 mg/kg/day (3-mo oral, reversible effects)
Cyclopentyl methyl ether15 mg/dayClass 2 · 1500 ppm · NOEL 150 mg/kg/day (28-day oral)
Tertiary-butyl alcohol35 mg/dayClass 2 · 3500 ppm · LOEL 175 mg/kg/day (2-yr rat nephropathy)
2-MTHF: 250 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 5 × 1 × 1) = 50 mg/day → Class 3 (PDE = 50) CPME: 150 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 10 × 1 × 1) = 15 mg/day → Class 2, limit 1500 ppm TBA: 175 × 50 / (5 × 10 × 1 × 1 × 5) = 35 mg/day → Class 2, limit 3500 ppm (mouse thyroid-hyperplasia scenario for TBA gives 42.5 mg/day / 4250 ppm)

TBA’s male-rat kidney tumours are α2u-globulin-nephropathy-mediated (not relevant to humans), so the female-rat nephropathy LOEL was used instead.

Case 9 · Worked PDE derivation — acetonitrile (Appendix 3 example)

Appendix 3

Mouse study (Pharmeuropa 1997, S24): NOEL = 50.7 mg/kg/day. This is the canonical worked example of the Appendix 3 method:

PDE = (50.7 mg/kg/day × 50 kg) / (12 × 10 × 5 × 1 × 1) = 2535 / 600 = 4.22 ≈ 4.1 mg/day (Table 2) Option 1 limit = 4.1 × 1000 / 10 = 410 ppm

F1=12 (mice→humans), F2=10 (inter-individual), F3=5 (13-week study), F4=1 (no severe toxicity), F5=1 (NOEL determined).

Glossary (from the guideline)

TermDefinition
PDE — Permitted Daily ExposureMaximum acceptable intake per day of residual solvent in pharmaceutical products.
NOEL — No-Observed-Effect LevelHighest dose with no biologically significant increases in frequency/severity of effects in exposed humans or animals.
LOEL — Lowest-Observed-Effect LevelLowest dose producing biologically significant increases in frequency/severity of any effects.
TDI / ADITolerable (IPCS) / acceptable (WHO) daily intake — PDE is the pharmaceutically-specific analogue to avoid ADI confusion.
Genotoxic carcinogenCarcinogen that produces cancer by affecting genes or chromosomes.
Strongly suspected human carcinogenNo epidemiological evidence, but positive genotoxicity data and clear rodent carcinogenicity evidence.
Neurotoxicity / TeratogenicityAdverse effects on the nervous system / structural malformations in a developing fetus.
Reversible toxicityHarmful effects that disappear after exposure ends.
Modifying factorFactor determined by toxicologist judgement applied to bioassay data (F1–F5).
